The clock hole is the only real hole in the paper — it's the sprocket track that drives the reader and is punched on every row, whether the data bit is 0 or 1. The second circle next to it isn't a hole: it's a cell a human fills in with ink to write the data bit — solid = 1, blank = 0. A blank 30mm feed buffer runs before the first row and after the last row of the whole tape — nothing to catch on a reader mechanism — labeled CLOCK and DATA with a feed-direction arrow so the strip only goes in one way. Layout follows the original Arduining.com punched-tape reader design — one clock pulse and one data bit per row, 8 rows per byte. Print at 100% / actual size — disable "fit to page" in your print dialog, or the tape will scale off-pitch.
